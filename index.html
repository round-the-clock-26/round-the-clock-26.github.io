Eight races on four continents, sixty-six hours of racing, two classes sharing one track. An unofficial tour of the 2026 FIA World Endurance Championship, from the first hour at Imola to the last at Monza.

The page keeps race time. Its clock is the one that ran at Le Mans in June: four on Saturday afternoon up here, through the night as you scroll, four on Sunday at the bottom. The sky and the light on the cars follow it.

17:00 Saturday

Racing against the clock

A Grand Prix is over inside two hours. A WEC race is measured in time, not laps: six hours at most rounds, twenty-four at Le Mans. Whoever has covered the most distance when the clock runs out wins, and nobody gets there alone.

Three drivers, one car

Every car is shared by two or three drivers who take turns behind the wheel. The FIA grades each of them Platinum, Gold, Silver or Bronze, and the grades decide who may race where.

Hypercar is for professionals: no Bronze drivers. LMGT3 is pro-am by rule: professionals share each car with amateurs. Every crew needs a Bronze driver plus a second Bronze or a Silver, and in a six-hour race each of them must drive at least an hour and three quarters.

Two classes, one track

Hypercars and LMGT3 cars race at the same time, each class with its own results and its own champions. At Le Mans an invited class of LMP2 cars, a cheaper tier of prototype, joins them, which put 62 cars on the 13.626 km Circuit de la Sarthe in June.

The winning Toyota covered 381 laps. The winning Corvette covered 336. Every one of those 45 laps was a moment when a Hypercar came up behind it and had to find a way past.

The longer the race, the more it pays

Points scale with the length of the race. A six-hour race pays 25 to the winner; Le Mans pays 50, and its tenth place is worth twice a six-hour tenth. Pole position, the fastest lap in qualifying, adds one point.

In 2026 that made Le Mans alone worth more than a fifth of the points a car could win all year. Seven six-hour races and one twenty-four-hour race added up to 66 hours of racing.

19:00 Saturday

Eight makers, two rulebooks

Hypercar admits two kinds of car. Balance of Performance adjusts their power and weight so either can win, and from 2026 the figures are no longer published, only shared with the teams.

LMH

Designed from scratch by its maker. An optional electric motor drives the front wheels, so a hybrid LMH has four-wheel drive above a set speed, around 190 km/h for Toyota and Ferrari in 2026.

Toyota, Ferrari, Peugeot, and the non-hybrid Aston Martin.

LMDh

Built on a chassis from one of four approved constructors, with a common 50 kW hybrid system on the rear axle. The engine and bodywork are the maker’s own.

BMW and Cadillac on Dallara chassis, Alpine and Genesis on Oreca.

20:50 Saturday

The other race

LMGT3 is for racing versions of road-going sports cars, built to the GT3 rules: eighteen of them from nine brands, run by private teams that buy their cars from the makers, and all on the same Goodyear tyres. They are much slower than Hypercars. At Le Mans the fastest LMGT3 car qualified in 3:52.433, about thirty seconds slower than the quickest Hypercar. The class trophy went to a 22-year-old in the Silver grade.

          Hypercar World Endurance Drivers’ Champions

          Sébastien Buemi Brendon Hartley Ryō Hirakawa

          Toyota

          215 points, four wins including Le Mans, and the title sealed at Barcelona with a round to spare. Toyota’s eighth manufacturers’ title since the WEC began in 2012.

          Jonny Edgar

          107 points. The 22-year-old from Cumbria was the only driver to race every round in the #33 Corvette, and clinched it with third place at Monza after a drive-through penalty dropped him to fifteenth.
